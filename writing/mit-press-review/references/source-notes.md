# Source Notes and Publication Context

Research checked: **2026-09-08**. These notes summarize MIT Press's public book guidance consulted on that date. Some linked PDFs have older dates in their filenames. This is a selective reference, not a complete house-style manual; use the current author hub to locate replacements when submission requirements matter.

Read only the sections relevant to the request. The diagnostics in `SKILL.md` are practical applications of these sources, not quotations from an internal reviewer questionnaire.

## Public Evaluation Criteria

### Book proposals

Source: [Submitting a Book Proposal](https://mitpress.mit.edu/submitting-book-proposal/).

MIT Press requests evidence of clear, precise prose and an appealing explanatory approach. It asks authors to establish the book's reader benefit, intended audience and prerequisites, distinctive features, relationship to competing titles, and teaching approach. A detailed contents list and sample chapters help assess execution.

These are acquisition and development criteria. They do not prescribe one prose voice, paragraph pattern, or universal level of technical detail. A proposal's market case is relevant to publishing decisions; do not equate market size with writing quality.

### Peer review

Source: [Peer Review](https://mitpress.mit.edu/peer-review/), public statement dated February 2018.

Books generally receive expert review at proposal and complete-manuscript stages. The stated evaluation dimensions include quality, originality, and distinctive contribution; textbooks may also be assessed for teaching value. Authors ordinarily respond to substantive criticism and explain intended revisions.

The page describes an open-ended questionnaire but does not publish its complete questions, weights, or acceptance thresholds. Do not invent these or imply that a prose review establishes scholarly novelty.

## General Author and Editing Guidance

Source: [Current Authors](https://mitpress.mit.edu/for-authors/).

The Press favors the latest Chicago Manual of Style while emphasizing consistent use of an accepted publication style. References must be complete and consistent, in-text author–date citations must have matching reference entries, and submitted URLs must be checked. Authors supply image alt text; a caption alone does not substitute for it.

**AI policy, only when relevant to the actual publishing context:** authors must disclose AI use to produce text or images/graphics or collect data, and remain responsible for their content. Do not infer AI use from prose style. MIT Press's policy explicitly states:

> Usage of large language models or other artificial intelligence tools to assist in the peer review process is strictly prohibited.

Keep author-facing drafting, self-review, and revision distinct from assisting with a formal commissioned MIT Press peer-review assignment. Do not present this skill as authorized assistance for that assignment; explain the policy and limit assistance to general guidance that does not evaluate the assigned manuscript. Do not append this policy to ordinary writing reviews unless the user's context makes it relevant.

## Book-Type Guidance

### General-audience trade books

Source: [Guidelines for Authors Writing Trade Books](https://dhjhkxawhe8q4.cloudfront.net/mit-press/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/26191651/Trade-guidelines-2023.pdf), especially pp. 2, 4–5.

- Use Chicago notes and bibliography, with numbered endnotes; do not use in-text author–date citations. A separate bibliography is unnecessary when the notes contain full bibliographic details.
- Tables and equations are strongly discouraged, but may be included sparingly when needed. Treat this as a preference, not an absolute prohibition.
- Use no more than two subheading levels; do not number subheadings.
- Authors review copyedits, answer queries, and verify accuracy. Finish content changes during copyedit review; typeset proofs allow correction of typos and other serious errors.

These requirements concern trade manuscripts and production stages. Do not impose them on every nonfiction excerpt. For an actual submission, consult the full current guide for file and figure requirements beyond this writing skill's scope.

### Scholarly and professional books

Source: [Scholarly and Professional Book Guidelines](https://dhjhkxawhe8q4.cloudfront.net/mit-press/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/15154057/MITPress_Author_Professional_Guidelines-2024.pdf), especially pp. 2–4.

- Notes and bibliography or author–date references are available systems. Use one consistently; general-audience books should use notes and bibliography.
- References must be accurate, complete, and consistently styled according to Chicago or an established journal in the field.
- Up to three subheading levels are permitted.

The trade PDF's two-level limit and the general guidance's three-level limit have different scopes. Use the guide appropriate to the book and any supplied editor instructions; do not report an unresolved scope difference as a definite violation.

### Essential Knowledge books

Sources: [Series description](https://mitpress.mit.edu/series/mit-press-essential-knowledge-series/) and [Author Guidelines](https://dhjhkxawhe8q4.cloudfront.net/mit-press/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/26191700/EKS-Guidelines_8-19.pdf), especially p. 2.

The series promises concise, accessible expert overviews for nonspecialists, building understanding of complex ideas from fundamentals. Apply that audience promise to the depth and sequence of explanations; concision must retain the concepts needed to understand the subject.

The preparation guide specifies an introduction as chapter 1, a glossary with at least 15–20 terms, further readings, and notes-and-bibliography documentation rather than in-text author–date references. These are series-specific requirements, not mandatory components of every MIT Press book.

### Abstracts

Source: [Guidelines for Preparing Abstracts and Keywords](https://mitpress.mit.edu/guidelines-preparing-abstracts-and-keywords/).

Book abstracts should identify the main arguments and conclusions, target about 200 words, and stay within 250. Chapter abstracts target about 120 words and stay within 150. The page prefers an impersonal voice in these abstracts. Do not extend that preference into a prohibition on first-person narration throughout a book.

## Attribution Limits

Ordinary craft decisions—moving a definition earlier, splitting an overloaded sentence, replacing a vague transition, or cutting repetition—need a demonstrated reader benefit. They are editorial recommendations informed by the public criteria, not independent MIT Press rules.

Do not treat books published by the Press, general MIT writing resources, or a single editor's personal preferences as press-wide policy. For live policy questions, follow the official page or the applicable guide linked from the author hub and identify unresolved differences.
