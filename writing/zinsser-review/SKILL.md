# Zinsser Review

Apply Zinsser's craft as an editorial lens: make the writer's meaning easier to follow, remove what does no useful work, and preserve the person behind the prose.

## Load the Principles

Read [references/zinsser-principles.md](references/zinsser-principles.md) completely for every review. It contains the four core principles, the broader *On Writing Well* framework, form-specific checks, guardrails, and source basis.

Call Clarity, Simplicity, Brevity, and Humanity Zinsser's four principles or articles of faith. A user may call them four laws, but do not imply that Zinsser presented a rigid mechanical rulebook.

## Establish the Editorial Contract

Infer these inputs from the request and document. Do not block when one is unavailable.

- Deliverable: findings, annotated edits, a clean rewrite, coaching, or a before/after comparison
- Purpose: what the piece must help the reader understand, feel, decide, or do
- Audience and assumed knowledge
- Nonfiction form and publishing context
- Voice, dialect, tone, and house-style constraints to preserve
- Facts, quotations, legal language, terminology, and formatting that must remain exact

Default to findings plus representative revisions. Do not silently rewrite an entire work unless asked.

## Review in Passes

Keep the passes separate so sentence polish does not conceal a structural problem.

1. **Intention and meaning**: State the piece's apparent purpose in one sentence. Flag passages whose thought is not yet settled or whose intention conflicts with the draft.
2. **Clarity**: Trace the logic in reader order. Check referents, actors, actions, chronology, transitions, one-thought-per-sentence pressure, and the next fact the reader needs.
3. **Simplicity and clutter**: Find needless qualifiers, repetitions, throat-clearing, abstract noun clusters, jargon, inflated diction, roundabout constructions, and decoration that hides the point.
4. **Brevity and selection**: Remove material that does no necessary work. Compress without deleting meaning, evidence, rhythm, necessary qualification, or humane detail.
5. **Humanity and voice**: Check whether the prose sounds like a recognizable person. Restore honest stance, concrete people and action, warmth, confidence, curiosity, and appropriate first person where flattening has occurred.
6. **Words and sentences**: Prefer precise familiar words, concrete nouns, and active verbs when they improve the sentence. Check sentence energy, rhythm, variety, punctuation, usage, and how the prose sounds aloud.
7. **Unity and organization**: Test unity of purpose, scope, viewpoint, tense, tone, and form. Verify that each paragraph advances one coherent line and that the whole piece has a discoverable shape.
8. **Lead, progression, and ending**: Test whether the opening earns attention and establishes direction, whether each paragraph creates the need for the next, and whether the ending arrives after the work is done.
9. **Form-specific craft**: Apply the relevant interview, travel, memoir, science/technology, workplace, sports, arts/criticism, column, or humor checks from the reference.
10. **Rewrite and read aloud**: Recheck every proposed change for meaning, voice, sound, pace, and unintended overcorrection.

## Make Edits

- Make the smallest change that solves the identified problem.
- Preserve the writer's facts, implications, emotional temperature, and characteristic voice.
- Prefer showing a strong alternative over issuing an abstract command.
- Separate structural decisions from line edits; a polished paragraph may still be unnecessary or misplaced.
- Query ambiguity rather than deciding what the writer meant.
- Treat technical terms, intentional repetition, passive voice, long sentences, and specialized diction as contextual choices, not automatic errors.
- Never use a target word count or readability score as a substitute for editorial judgment.

When asked for a rewrite, complete a purpose-and-structure pass before compressing sentences. After revising, compare the result with the source and restore any lost fact, caveat, transition, voice marker, or useful detail.

## Report Findings

Lead with the piece's apparent intention and the two or three changes with the greatest reader impact.

For each finding, provide:

1. Principle or craft area
2. Location or short identifying excerpt
3. Reader problem
4. Proposed edit or action
5. Why it improves the draft without erasing the voice

Use these labels:

- **Structural**: purpose, scope, selection, sequence, lead, ending, or form problem
- **Required for clarity**: meaning is ambiguous, contradictory, or materially hard to follow
- **Strong edit**: clear improvement in simplicity, brevity, humanity, or flow
- **Polish**: optional improvement in rhythm, diction, or emphasis
- **Query**: authorial intention or factual meaning must be resolved first

Group repeated habits into one pattern finding with representative examples. End with:

- Strengths worth preserving
- Checks completed, including areas with no findings
- Unresolved author decisions
- A compact revised passage when useful or requested

## Combine with Other Standards

Use Zinsser as a craft lens, not a house style. When another style guide, legal requirement, accessibility rule, or domain convention applies, preserve its mandatory choices and use this skill to improve the prose within those constraints. Name the source of each recommendation when rules conflict.
